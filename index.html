    Audit log

      Framing

      The trace isn't the product. The decision is.

      An enterprise agent run has two readers. A claims adjuster needs to decide in under a minute whether to trust this payout. An AI engineer needs to find why the agent stalled. Most trace views are built for the second reader and handed to the first. I designed for the adjuster first and put the engineer's detail one click away.

      Lead with the outcome and why it paused
      The top card says what the agent wants to do, which rules stopped it, and links to the exact step. Tradeoff: raw tool calls and tokens are hidden by default. The builder view brings them back without changing the layout.
      Confidence is the weakest step, not an average
      Four steps at 0.9+ and one at 0.62 would average to a comforting 0.88. A payout is only as sound as its weakest input, so the run shows the lowest step and points to it.
      Three responses, not approve or reject
      Approve, correct the amount, or ask the agent to re-check one step. Re-checking keeps the person directing the agent instead of redoing its work by hand, and only that step re-runs.
      A correction needs a reason, and becomes a test case
      Tradeoff: this adds friction to overriding. Without it, an override teaches the system nothing and can't be audited. With it, each correction becomes a test case for the next version of the agent.
      Guardrails are visible where they fired
      PII redaction and the approval rule show inline on their steps, so compliance can see the controls ran without opening a separate log.

      Where this could break

      Do reviewers read 0.62, or just the colour?
      I show both the number and a label. I'd test whether adjusters act differently at 0.62 and 0.74, and whether the number is calibrated enough to deserve two decimal places.
      Longer runs and parallel agents
      Six steps fit a list. A 200-step run with agents working in parallel needs grouping by agent and a collapsed "nothing unusual here" state, or the weak step gets buried.
      Pause fatigue
      If most runs pause, reviewers start approving without reading. I'd track how long a review takes and how often a pause ends in a change, and tune the thresholds from that.
      Where this comes from
      This extends my Activity Log case study, where I designed a log of every recruiter and candidate action for an HR platform. The question is the same one layer up: what happened, who did it, and can I trust it.
